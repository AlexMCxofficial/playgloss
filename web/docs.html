How to Play

PlayGloss is an English guess-the-meaning game for one to four players sharing one screen. Spin the wheel, get a word, pick the right meaning, and be the first to reach 100 points.

A turn

  1. Spin the wheel. It lands on a level or on a power-up.
  2. On a level, you see its points and time. If you want, choose a power-up (2x, 3x or Shield) now, before you see the word.
  3. Read the word and pick the right meaning before the timer runs out.
  4. A right answer gives you the points. A wrong answer or time up gives none (Shield gives half).

The wheel looks evenly divided, but the odds are not: easier levels come up far more often. Every level has its own 1,400 words.

Power-ups

  • 2x doubles your points if you are right (1.02%).
  • 3x triples them (0.68%).
  • Shield gives half the points if you are wrong (2.56%).
  • Free 5 gives 5 points straight away (0.34%).

You choose 2x, 3x or Shield after you see the level and before you see the word. You can use one power-up per question, and it is used up either way. There is also a very rare Easter egg. Good luck.

Winning

When someone reaches 100 points, the round finishes so everyone gets the same number of turns. The higher score wins, and a tie goes to a sudden-death spin.

Switching pages

Open Home or Documentation and come back, and your game is still there for as long as the tab or window stays open.

Two switches change the rules of any mode: Extreme flips the odds, and Infinite replaces the 100-point goal with hearts. Turn them on in Settings → Modes.

Names and Colors

Before a game each player picks a name and a color.

  • Color: choose from 16 presets, type a hex code, use the RGB sliders or drag around the color wheel. The two sides cannot have the same color or a look-alike color.
  • Name: up to 16 characters, with bold, italic and underline if you like.
  • Offensive names are refused with a red message. The check is basic: it works offline, in English only, and it cannot catch everything.

Turn Order

In 1 vs 1 and 2 vs 2, a random draw from 0 to 9 decides who goes first. The play order is shown in a Turn order list, and the player whose turn it is gets a highlight.

On phones and narrow windows the Turn order list is on the Players tab at the bottom of the screen. A player who resigned, or who has lost every heart in Infinite mode, is out and is skipped.

Solo

Just you, racing to 100 points. When you get there, the game tells you how many turns it took. There is no resign and no draw.

1 vs 1

Two players share one screen. Each has a card showing name, color, score and power-ups, and their own Offer draw and Resign buttons. A draw only happens when the other player accepts.

2 vs 2

Two teams of two. A Team Scoreboard shows each team's combined score, and a leaderboard shows each player's share. A draw offer is for everyone, but resigning is only for you.

Custom

The Play page has a Custom tab, left of Online. Custom is played by two teams of two, like 2 vs 2, and it works on phones and iPads. Its settings are in tabs:

  • General: Default or Extreme, and Infinite on or off.
  • Time: the time to answer for each level. Very Easy gives the most time, Impossible the least.
  • Colors: the color of each level on the wheel.
  • Teams: team names.
  • Hearts or target score: 1 to 10 hearts with Infinite, or a target score from 100 to 1000 without it.

With Advanced Mode on, an Advanced tab appears before Teams. A progress bar above each name fills as you get closer to the goal. With Infinite it starts full and loses one share for every lost heart. It shows no number.

Before every Start game or Host a game with Custom rules, PlayGloss asks “Would you like to export this configuration?”. Tick “Don't show this again” to stop it.

Infinite

There is no 100-point goal. Each player gets three Minecraft-style hearts, and a wrong answer or time up costs a heart. The game goes on until the hearts are gone.

  • Solo: the game ends when you lose your last heart.
  • 1 vs 1 and 2 vs 2: a player with no hearts is out and is skipped, like after resigning. When every player of one side is out, the other side plays on until it passes the score the first side ended with (then it wins) or loses its own hearts (then the higher score wins, and the same score is a draw).

Infinite works with Default and Extreme, and in Custom you can set 1 to 10 hearts. The heart picture is listed in the credits.

Extreme

Extreme flips the level odds: Impossible comes up as often as Very Easy does in Default, Insanely Hard as often as Easy, and so on. Points, time per level and the Easter egg odds do not change.

Extreme mode uses the same word list as Default. Mode and color are separate: choosing Extreme mode picks the Extreme color (dark red) automatically, but you can still change either one.

Online

Play with other people on the same Wi-Fi or network. One person hosts from the PlayGloss app and picks Default, Extreme or Custom and Infinite in the Host a game tab. Everyone else joins with Join a game, and sees the same rules.

The host can also set the guests' Color to Extreme: every guest gets the dark red look, the host's own look never changes, and guests can still switch to light.

See Online Play for the steps.

General

Open Settings with the gear at the top left. All choices are saved only on your device and are never sent anywhere.

Theme and color

Choose Light or Dark, and a color scheme: PlayGloss, OLED (pure black), Yaru (Ubuntu orange), Extreme (dark red) or Custom (pick your own accent color). OLED and Extreme are dark-only: choosing one switches to Dark, and choosing Light leaves it.

Fonts

Seven fonts: System (your device's own font, the default), Helvetica, Arial, Segoe UI, Compasse, Fira Code and JetBrains Mono. A size slider goes from 75% to 150% (100% is the default) and scales the whole app. Fira Code and JetBrains Mono are shipped with PlayGloss. The other four show only where they are installed, otherwise a similar font is used.

Background

  • Scheme: the color scheme's own background (the default).
  • Color: one solid color from the color picker. Text and cards adapt so they stay readable.
  • Image: your own picture. It is shrunk to fit a screen and kept only on your device. Fit Cover fills the screen and crops, Contain shows the whole picture. Blur goes from 0 (off) to 10.

Choose Image, then pick a picture. The Image button stays highlighted while you choose, and your earlier background stays in place until the picture is ready.

Documentation navigation

Under Documentation you choose whether the navigation sits on the Left or the Right. On large screens (1024 pixels wide and up) it is a sidebar, and the sidebar icon at the top right of the Documentation page switches it on or off. On tablets and phones the navigation is a drawer that opens with the Contents button. The Left/Right choice is greyed out on phones, where the drawer is always on the left.

Modes

Modes change the rules of the game. They are off by default, so you have to turn them on yourself.

  • Mode: Default or Extreme.
  • Infinite: on or off. See Infinite.

When you host an online game, you pick its mode in the Host a game tab instead.

Advanced Mode

The last section of Settings. It is off by default and changes nothing until you turn it on and switch on Custom rules on the Play page. Then an Advanced tab appears before Teams with:

  • Probabilities: the chance of each of the 8 levels and of 2x, 3x, Shield and Free 5, in percent with up to two decimals. They must add up to exactly 100%. The page always shows how much is left to give out and the most each item can still take. Extreme mode still flips the eight level chances. The Easter egg's chance cannot be changed.
  • Enable/Disable “?”: switches the surprise slice on or off.
  • Questions: Default (the built-in words) or Custom (your own multiple-choice questions with 2, 3 or 4 choices, up to 1,000). Import and export them as a CSV word list: word,meaning,wrong1,wrong2,wrong3,choices.
  • Advanced Custom Mode Configurations: export or import the whole Custom setup plus the Advanced settings as one JSON file. Saving a file, sharing it or copying the text all work in the apps and in the browser.

Play Online

The Same network switch only unlocks hosting. It is off by default, and nothing listens on the network while it is off.

  • Hosting needs the PlayGloss app for Windows, macOS, Linux or Android, so the switch stays off in the browser.
  • Joining does not need this switch: use Online, then Join a game, on the Play page.
  • Turning it on does not open anything by itself. A game can only be joined after you choose Online, then Host a game. Guest approval is required by default, and the room closes when you stop hosting or leave the page.
  • Only use it on a network you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi, and not on public Wi-Fi. Windows and macOS may ask you to allow network access the first time you host.

How to Host

  1. In the PlayGloss app, turn on Settings → Play Online.
  2. On the Play page choose Online, then Host a game.
  3. Choose the rules: Default, Extreme or Custom, and Infinite on or off. Optionally set the guests' Color to Extreme.
  4. Show the QR code or share the link with the people on your network.
  5. Approve each person who asks to join, then start the game.

The server stops when you press Stop, leave the page, close the app or the game ends.

Kahoot-like Hosting

On Windows, macOS or Linux, turn on Advanced Mode and Play Online in Settings. Open Play → Online → Host a game, turn on Custom rules, then select Advanced → Layout → Kahoot-like. Traditional is the default. Website and Android players can join from the invite link or QR code.

The host approves or removes players in the waiting lobby. With Approve Guests off, anyone on the same network with the invite link can join while a place is available. Start with at least two connected players. Everyone answers on their own devices.

  • With Difficulty Tiers on, spin once per question. Each player gets a different question at the same tier, with the same points and time limit. Use the player selector to view individual questions on the host screen.
  • With Difficulty Tiers off, add 1–1,000 custom questions in the order you want to ask them. Set the answer format, 1–1,000 points and an optional 2× or 3× multiplier for each. Each question has 20 seconds. Use the separate question-set import and export in this tab to reuse a list.
  • Choose multiple choice, tick box with one or more correct answers, or matching pairs. Preview Question gives players 5–20 seconds to read the prompt before choices appear. Timed Scores awards 50–100% of the available points for a correct answer, based on response time.
  • See the answer distribution after each round. If enabled, Show leaderboard displays the top five with rank changes and optional streaks. A streak starts after three correct answers in a row. At the end, Show podium displays up to three finishers.

Use Export/Import in the spectator settings to move the full setup, including the question list and wheel settings. Close room returns you to setup and ends the connection for everyone.

How to Join

Joining works in the app and on the website, on the same network as the host, and needs no switch.

  1. On the Play page choose Online, then Join a game.
  2. Paste the link, or scan the host's QR code. You can also scan it with any camera app, or open the link in any browser.
  3. If the host requires approval, wait for them to let you in.

PlayGloss only connects to a private network address (such as 192.168.x.x or 10.x.x.x). A link to anything else is refused. The in-app QR scanner needs a system or browser with a built-in QR reader, for example Chrome on Android.

Connection Types

The lobby's Connection section helps guests get onto the same network as the host:

  • Open Wi-Fi: no password, guests just connect.
  • Password Wi-Fi: you can show the Wi-Fi name and password, with an optional Wi-Fi QR code.
  • Login-page network: for example at a school, where you sign in on a web page. You can note the Wi-Fi name, a username (for example ssxxxxx) and the password on the host's screen.

Wi-Fi names, usernames and passwords are not stored or sent by PlayGloss. They stay on the host's screen only while the lobby is open.

Privacy and Safety

  • Online play only works on a local network (Wi-Fi or LAN). Nothing goes over the internet.
  • Game messages are encrypted with the secret in the link. The host's server and the join client only pass them along and never read them.
  • The camera is used only after you press the scan button and only while the preview is open. Nothing is saved or sent.
  • Paste link reads your clipboard only when you press it, once, and keeps nothing.
  • The host's rules (mode, Infinite and Custom settings) are sent to guests so everybody plays by the same rules. They contain nothing about you.

Windows

  1. Download the .exe installer from the Releases page (64-bit for most PCs, 32-bit only for old 32-bit Windows 10).
  2. The app is not signed, so Windows may show “Windows protected your PC”. Choose More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer, then open PlayGloss from the Start menu.

If you host an online game, Windows may ask you to allow network access the first time. Allow it on private networks only.

macOS

  1. Download the .dmg, open it and drag PlayGloss to Applications.
  2. The app is not signed. The first time, Control-click (or right-click) PlayGloss and choose Open, then Open again.
  3. If macOS still blocks it, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway.

This build has not been tested on a real Mac yet. Please report any problem.

Linux

  • .deb (Debian 12+, Ubuntu 22.04+): open it with your software installer, or run sudo apt install ./PlayGloss*.deb.
  • .AppImage: make it executable (chmod +x PlayGloss*.AppImage) and run it. Some systems need FUSE (libfuse2) for AppImages.

Only 64-bit Intel and AMD systems are supported.

Android

  1. Download the .apk from the Releases page (Android 7.0 or newer).
  2. Open it. Android asks you to allow installing apps from this source (your browser or file manager). Allow it.
  3. Tap Install, then open PlayGloss.

You can turn the “unknown sources” permission off again afterwards.

iPhone and iPad

There is no App Store app. Use the website as a web app instead:

  1. Open the website in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button.
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen.

The game is kept on the device after the first visit, so it works offline. iPhones and iPads can join online games, but only the apps for Windows, macOS, Linux and Android can host one.

Privacy Policy

PlayGloss collects nothing.

  • No analytics, cookies, ads or trackers, and no accounts. The game makes no network requests while you play.
  • The only things stored are your settings, kept on your own device and never sent anywhere: theme, color scheme, mode, fonts, background, documentation navigation, and the Advanced Mode settings.
  • A picture, a configuration file or a word list you choose is read only on your device and is never uploaded.
  • Scores, power-ups and answers exist only while the page is open.
  • Online play stays on your own local network, and the camera and clipboard are used only when you press the matching button.
