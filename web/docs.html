Documentation

How to play

PlayGloss is an English vocabulary game for one to four players sharing one screen. Each turn a player spins the wheel and tries to pick the correct meaning of a word. Reach 100 points first to win.

Game modes

  • Solo: just you. Keep spinning until you reach 100 points. There is no other team, so no resign or draw, and the game tells you how many turns it took.
  • 1 vs 1: two players take turns. Each has a card beside the wheel with their name, color, score and power-ups, and their own Offer draw and Resign buttons below it. A random draw decides who goes first.
  • 2 vs 2: two teams of two. Every player has a card with their own score and power-ups. A Team Scoreboard above the wheel shows each team's combined score, and a leaderboard beside the wheel shows how much each player adds to their team. Read Turn order in 2 vs 2 below.
  • Custom: a fully configurable game mode. You choose the Extreme and Infinite switches, hearts per player (in Infinite mode), target score (in non-Infinite mode), time multipliers for each difficulty level, and custom colors for the wheel slices. Custom games use the 2 vs 2 team structure (two teams, two players each) and support all the same features. See Custom mode below.
  • Online: play 1 vs 1 or 2 vs 2 with people on the same Wi-Fi or network, each on their own device. See Online play below.

Names and colors

  • Before a game starts you can choose a name (or a team name and player names in 2 vs 2). Each name has Bold, Italic and Underline buttons next to it, and the style shows wherever that name appears in the game. This works in all three modes.
  • Names can be up to 16 characters. Obviously offensive names, including disguised ones, are refused with a red message. This check runs on your device and is basic, so it cannot catch everything.
  • Pick any color you like. The Presets tab has 16 colors: White, Light Gray, Gray, Black, Brown, Red, Orange, Yellow, Lime, Green, Cyan, Light Blue, Blue, Purple, Magenta and Pink. The Hex tab takes a code such as #2f80ed (or #28e), the RGB tab has a slider and a number for red, green and blue, and the Wheel tab lets you tap or drag on a color wheel and set the brightness below it.
  • The two sides cannot have the same color, or one that looks almost the same. A preset that is too close to the other side's color is crossed out, and a color you type or drag to is refused with a message.

A turn

  1. The player whose turn it is spins the wheel. Turn order explains who goes first.
  2. The wheel lands on a difficulty level or a power-up.
  3. If it lands on a power-up, you keep it and the turn is over. Each player keeps their own power-ups.
  4. If it lands on a level, you see the level first. If you own a 2x, 3x or Shield, you may use one now, before the word appears.
  5. The word appears with four possible meanings and a countdown. Pick one before time runs out.

Power-ups

  • You use 2x, 3x and Shield after you see the level and before you see the word.
  • Only one power-up can be active on a question.
  • A power-up is used up whether you answer right, wrong or run out of time. A 2x you did not get right is lost.
  • Unused power-ups stay with you for later turns.

The wheel is drawn with equal slices, but the odds above are what really decide the result. Percentages are rounded, and a tiny remainder is left for something very rare.

Winning

  • Solo: you win as soon as you reach 100 points (in Custom, your target score).
  • 1 vs 1 and 2 vs 2: when a team reaches 100 or more, the round is finished first, so everyone gets the same number of turns. The round ends after the last player in the turn order has played. Then the higher team score wins. In 2 vs 2 a team's score is its two players' scores added together.
  • If the scores are tied, each team spins once more in sudden death (in 2 vs 2, the first remaining player of each team spins). The higher result wins, where a level counts as its points, Free 5 counts as 5 and other power-ups count as 0. A tie spins again.

Extreme mode

Extreme is a mode you turn on yourself in Settings, under Modes. It flips how often the levels come up: Impossible comes up as often as Very Easy does in Default, Insanely Hard as often as Easy, and so on. Only the odds change. Points and time per level stay the same (Impossible still gives 15 points and 30 seconds), the power-ups keep their chances, and the Easter egg is exactly as rare as before.

Choosing Extreme in Settings also switches the color to Extreme (dark red with a red accent). Choosing Default again puts your old look back, unless you changed it yourself. You can still use the Extreme color in Default mode, and the other way round.

Infinite mode

Infinite is a switch in Settings, under Modes, and it works with Default and Extreme. There is no 100 points goal. Every player has 3 hearts (in Custom you choose 1 to 10), and a wrong answer or running out of time costs one heart (a Shield still gives its points, but the heart is lost). A player with no hearts left is out and is skipped, like after resigning.

  • Solo: the game ends when your hearts are gone, and it shows your points and turns.
  • 1 vs 1 and 2 vs 2: when everyone on one side is out, the other side plays on. It wins as soon as it has a higher score than the side that is out. If it runs out of hearts first, the higher score wins, and the same score is a draw. There are no rounds and no sudden death. Offer draw is off once the other side is out, but Resign still works.

Custom mode

Custom mode lets you set up the rules yourself. Open the Play page and choose Custom (it sits just left of Online). The settings are in four tabs that fit on a phone or an iPad (a fifth tab, Advanced, appears before Teams when Advanced Mode is on in Settings):

  • General: choose Default or Extreme (Extreme flips how often the levels come up) and switch Infinite on or off. With Infinite on, a slider sets the hearts per player (1 to 10) and there is no target score. With Infinite off, a slider sets the target score (100 to 1000, in steps of 50).
  • Time: the time to answer, from Very Easy (the most time) to Impossible (the least). Normal is the standard game. A table shows the seconds each level gets in your game.
  • Colors: the color of each of the eight levels on the wheel. This changes the look only, not the odds, points or time. Reset to default colors brings the originals back.
  • Teams: names, text styles and colors. Custom is played by two teams of two, like 2 vs 2, with the same turn order, draw and resign rules.

Custom works offline and online: host a Custom game from Online then Host a game in the app, and guests see your hearts or target score, your wheel colors and your time to answer.

Progress bars

The bar above each team or player name is a progress bar. It shows no number. In Default, Extreme and Custom without Infinite it fills from left to right as you get closer to the goal: 50 of 100 points fills half of it, and in Custom the goal is your target score. A team's bar counts both players' points. With Infinite it works the other way round: the bar starts full and every lost heart takes away one share of it (a third with 3 hearts, a fifth with 5 hearts, a tenth with 10 hearts). On a player's own card in Infinite the bar shows that player's hearts, and on a team box it shows all of the team's hearts.

Turn order

The game decides who plays first with a random draw before every 1 vs 1 and 2 vs 2 game. The result is final and cannot be changed. It is shown in a Turn order list below the Spin button (on a phone, in the Players tab), and the player whose turn it is is marked. Solo has no list.

1 vs 1

Each player draws a random number from 0 to 9. The higher number goes first. If the numbers are equal, both draw again.

2 vs 2

  1. Every player draws a random number from 0 to 9.
  2. Each team adds up its two numbers. The team with the higher total goes first. If both totals are equal, everyone draws again.
  3. Inside each team, the player with the higher number goes first. If the two teammates drew the same number, only those two draw again, as many times as it takes, until the numbers are different.
  4. The team that goes first plays both of its players, higher number first. Then the other team plays both of its players, higher number first. After that the order repeats.

Example: Red is Alice (9) and Bob (8), a total of 17. Blue is Cara (9) and Dan (9), a total of 18. Blue has the higher total, so Blue goes first. Cara and Dan are tied, so they draw again and get 6 and 7. Dan has the higher number, so Dan goes before Cara. Alice drew more than Bob, so she goes before him. The play order is Dan, Cara, Alice, Bob.

Resign and draw

As in chess, on your turn, before you spin, you can resign or offer a draw. The buttons are under your own card and are grayed out when you cannot use them, for example when it is not your turn. You can offer one draw per turn. Solo has neither button.

  • 1 vs 1: resigning gives the win to the other player. If the other player accepts a draw, the game ends as a draw.
  • 2 vs 2: a draw offer is made for everyone, and if the other team accepts, the game ends as a draw for all four players. Resigning is only for you: your turns are skipped from then on and your points stay in your team's total. If your teammate does not resign, your team keeps playing. If both teammates resign, the other team wins.

Leaderboard in 2 vs 2

Each team has a leaderboard beside the wheel. It lists the team's players from highest to lowest and shows each player's points and what percentage of the team's score they earned. On a phone it is on the Leaderboard tab at the bottom.

Switching pages and phones

  • If you open Home or Documentation and come back to Play, your game, names, colors and scores are still there. They are kept only for as long as that browser tab or app window stays open. Closing it starts fresh.
  • If you leave in the middle of a spin or a question, that turn is skipped with no points, and any power-up you had selected is given back.
  • On small screens the play page is split into tabs at the bottom: Game, Players (the player cards and the turn order) and, in 2 vs 2, Leaderboard.

Settings

The gear at the top left opens Settings. Under General you can choose:

  • Theme: Light or Dark. The moon and sun button at the top right does the same thing.
  • Color: PlayGloss (the default look), OLED (pure black for dark screens), Yaru (Ubuntu orange with warm grays), Extreme (dark red with a red accent) or Custom.
  • Custom uses the same color picker as the team colors, but it only changes the accent color used for buttons and highlights. Text on the accent color turns black or white automatically so it stays readable.

Under Fonts you can choose:

  • Font: System (your device's own font, the default), Helvetica, Arial, Segoe UI, Compasse, Fira Code and JetBrains Mono. Fira Code and JetBrains Mono are open source (SIL Open Font License), come with PlayGloss and are monospace fonts. Helvetica, Arial, Segoe UI and Compasse are not shipped with PlayGloss (they are proprietary, and Compasse is a commercial font), so they only show if your device has them; otherwise a similar font is used.
  • Size: a slider from 75% to 150% (100% is the default). You can also type a number. The whole app changes at once.

Under Background you can choose how the app background looks:

  • Scheme (the default): the background of your color scheme.
  • Color: one solid color, picked with the same color picker as the team colors (presets, hex, RGB sliders and the color wheel). Text, cards and borders switch to black or white shades automatically so everything stays readable.
  • Image: a picture from your device. PlayGloss shrinks it so its longest side is at most 1920 pixels and keeps it only on your device; it is never uploaded. Cover fills the whole screen and crops the edges, Contain shows the whole picture and fills the rest with the scheme color.
  • Blur (Image only): from 0 (off, the default) to 10 (strongest).

Under Modes you can choose Default or Extreme, and switch Infinite on or off. See Extreme mode and Infinite mode above. Both are off by default.

Advanced Mode is the last part of Settings. It is off by default and does nothing until you also switch on Custom rules on the Play page. Then an Advanced tab appears before Teams:

  • Probabilities: set the chance of each of the 8 levels and of 2x, 3x, Shield and Free 5, in percent with up to two decimals (the standard odds are filled in to start with; the first box shows 34.08% instead of 34.07% so that all twelve add up to exactly 100.00%). They must add up to exactly 100%. The page shows how much is left to give out (or how far over you are) and, for each row, the most it can still take. Odds are only used when they add up to 100%, and Start game or Host a game will tell you if they do not. Extreme mode still flips the eight level chances (what you give Very Easy goes to Impossible and so on); the power-ups stay as you set them.
  • Enable/Disable "?": turns the surprise slice on or off. Its own chance cannot be changed.
  • Questions: Default uses the built-in words. Custom asks your own questions: choose 2, 3 or 4 choices, type the word, the right meaning and the wrong ones, and add it (up to 1,000 questions). A random one is asked each turn, none comes back before all have been asked, and the level still decides the points and the time. You can import and export the list as a CSV word list. The format is word,meaning,wrong1,wrong2,wrong3,choices on the first line; every other line is one question (a 2-choice question only needs wrong1, a 3-choice one wrong1 and wrong2). Put text that contains a comma in double quotes. Importing replaces your list, and nothing changes if any line has a problem.
  • Advanced Custom Mode Configurations: Export or Import the whole Custom setup (mode, Infinite, hearts or target score, time, wheel colors) together with the probabilities, the "?" switch and your questions, as one JSON file. With Advanced Mode off, the same box is called Custom Mode Configurations and only holds the Custom setup. Exporting offers Save file (a download, or the share sheet on a phone), Copy to clipboard and the text itself, and importing takes a file or pasted text, so it works the same on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and in the browser.
  • Export question: before every Start game and Host a game with Custom rules (Advanced Mode on or off), PlayGloss asks "Would you like to export this configuration?". Tick "Don't show this again" to stop it; you can turn it back on in the Configurations box.

Everything you set here stays on your device. In an online game only the host's device uses the odds and the questions; guests only see the questions that are asked.

OLED and Extreme only exist in the dark theme, so choosing one switches to Dark, and choosing Light switches it off. Your settings stay on your device after you close the app. They are never sent anywhere, so nobody else can read them.

Download and install

You can always play in your browser on the Play page. Apps for offline play are published on the Releases page as they become available. They contain the whole game and need no internet connection.

Windows (.exe)

  1. Download the .exe from the Releases page and open it.
  2. If Windows shows a "protected your PC" warning because the app is not signed, choose More info, then Run anyway.

macOS (.dmg)

  1. Download the .dmg and open it.
  2. Drag PlayGloss to the Applications folder.
  3. The first time, macOS may say it cannot verify the app because it is not signed by Apple. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, scroll down and choose Open Anyway next to PlayGloss.
  4. If macOS says the app is damaged, run xattr -cr /Applications/PlayGloss.app in Terminal, then open it again.

Linux (.deb)

  1. Download the .deb.
  2. In a terminal, in the folder where it was saved, run sudo apt install ./FILENAME.deb with the real file name.

Linux (AppImage)

  1. Download the .AppImage file.
  2. Make it runnable: chmod +x PlayGloss*.AppImage
  3. Run it: ./PlayGloss*.AppImage

Android (.apk)

  1. Download the .apk on your phone and open it.
  2. If asked, allow your browser or file manager to install apps from this source.
  3. Tap Install.

iPhone and iPad (web app)

PlayGloss has no App Store version, but you can install it from the website. It then opens full screen with its own icon and works offline.

  1. Open the Play page in Safari and let it finish loading.
  2. Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open PlayGloss from your Home Screen. After that first load it works without internet.

The same steps work in Chrome on Android (menu, then Install app) and in desktop Chrome and Edge (install icon in the address bar).

Privacy

PlayGloss collects nothing. There are no accounts, analytics, cookies, ads or trackers, and the game makes no network requests while you play. Your settings (theme, color scheme and custom accent color) are saved only on your own device and are never sent anywhere, and if you install the website as a web app, your browser keeps a copy of the game's files so it works offline. Names, colors and scores are held in your browser tab's temporary session storage only so they survive a visit to another page, and they are erased when you close the tab or app. They are never sent anywhere. Read the full privacy notice.

Online play on the same network

The Play page has a fourth option next to Solo, 1 vs 1 and 2 vs 2: Online. It lets you host a game on your own Wi-Fi or local network, or join a game that someone else hosts. Hosting is opt-in and never runs unless you turn it on. Joining only connects to the address you give it.

How to host (app only)

  1. Open Settings and switch Play Online on. This only unlocks hosting; nothing listens on the network yet.
  2. Open the Play page, choose Online, then Host a game, pick 1 vs 1, 2 vs 2, or Custom, choose the Modes (Default or Extreme, and Infinite on or off; Custom also has hearts, target score, time to answer and wheel colors) and set the names. The mode you pick here is the one this game uses, whatever Settings says. Press Host a game. The app starts a small server on your local network and shows a clear "Online" indicator.
  3. The Modes box (or the General tab for Custom) also has a Color (guests) option: Default means guests keep their own look, Extreme puts the Extreme look (dark red) on every guest while your own look never changes. Guests can still switch to light, and switching back to dark brings Extreme back. It is only shown while they are in your game and is never saved on their device.
  4. In the lobby, the Connection box asks what kind of Wi-Fi you are on: No password, Password (a normal Wi-Fi) or Login page (school, hotel or cafe Wi-Fi). For the first two you can type the Wi-Fi name (and password) to show a Wi-Fi QR code that guests scan with their camera to join the Wi-Fi. For Login page, optional Wi-Fi name, Username / ID (at a school it is often something like ssxxxxx, where xxxxx is your own number) and Password fields let you keep the details on your screen (they are not saved, not sent to guests, and not part of any QR code). The name and password stay on your screen: they are not saved, not part of the join link and not sent to guests by PlayGloss. Login page networks often stop devices from talking to each other, so the box suggests logging in everywhere or using your phone's hotspot instead.
  5. Share the link shown on screen, or let guests scan the QR code. The secret part of the link (after the #) never leaves the devices that have the link.
  6. Each guest who asks to join appears in your lobby. You choose which seats guests can take ("Open for guest") and which you keep ("This device"), and you can remove anyone at any time.
  7. Press Start game when everyone is seated. Guests see the same game screen as you and spin, answer and draw on their turns.
  8. Press Stop hosting when you are done. The server stops, all guests are disconnected, and you are back to the setup screen.

How to join

  1. Connect to the same Wi-Fi or network as the host.
  2. Open the Play page, choose Online, then Join a game.
  3. Paste the join link (the Paste link button reads your clipboard only when you press it), or press Scan QR code and point the camera at the host's code. The scan button only appears where the app or browser has a built-in QR reader and camera access.
  4. Enter your name, wait for the host to let you in, and take a free seat.

You can also still join without the Online option: scan the host's QR code with any camera app, or open the link in any browser, including Safari on iPhone and iPad. Nothing needs to be installed.

In the app and on the website

  • App (Windows, macOS, Linux, Android): you can host and join. When you join, the app itself connects to the host, so you stay inside PlayGloss.
  • Website: you can join but not host. An https page cannot run a server or talk to an http address on your local network, so joining from the website opens the host's own join page in the same tab. Some browsers with a "HTTPS only" mode may warn you about that http address; the join page is on your own network, so you can continue if you trust it.

Privacy and safety

  • Nothing is collected and there are no accounts. Everything stays on your local network and is encrypted between the devices using the secret in the link.
  • A join link is only accepted if it points to a private network address (for example 192.168.x.x or 10.x.x.x). A link can never make PlayGloss connect to a server on the internet.
  • Scanning happens on your device. The camera picture is never saved or sent, and the camera is switched off as soon as you scan, cancel or leave the page.
  • Only use online play on a network you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi, not on public Wi-Fi.
  • Windows and macOS may ask you to allow network access the first time you host.
  • The game itself never sends anything over the internet.
